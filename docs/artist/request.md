# Commissioning a Secwépemc artist: draft request

The draft letter is below. Edit anything in [brackets]. It comes with:
- `seam-template.pdf`, two pages: (1) the table, the seam areas and the cutting rules in plain
  words; (2) a full seam at 1:4 with a grid, to sketch on directly.
- Optionally, a few screenshots from the project site (the 3-D view and one or two seam options),
  so they can see what "a seam" means in practice.

## Before you send it

- **Find the artist directly.** Look for a Secwépemc artist whose work you already like, rather than
  asking a band office to "assign" one. Places to ask or look (TODO-verify current contacts):
  - the Secwépemc Museum & Heritage Park in Kamloops;
  - the arts or culture staff of Secwépemc communities;
  - local Indigenous art markets and galleries;
  - artists' own websites and Instagram.
- **Pay for the conversation, not only the result.** Offer a fee for the first consultation, even if they
  then decide the project isn't a fit.
- **Be ready for "no", or for "not like that".** They may propose something different: a design that isn't
  a crest, a different subject, or that the design should come with its story, or without it.
- **Don't send our motif prototypes as "ideas".** The salmon and fern seams are generic nature shapes.
  Let the artist choose the subject.
- **Budget:** decide on a fee range first, and ask for their rate in the first message. It's a separate
  line from the ~$850–1300 plywood + heater estimate in PLAN.md.

## Draft letter

**Subject:** Commission inquiry: a seam design for a handmade table (paid)

Weyt-k [Name],

My name is [your name]. I live in [town], on Secwépemc land. I've admired your work, especially
[a specific piece or series], and I'm writing to ask whether you'd be interested in a small paid
commission.

**The project.** I'm making a *kotatsu*, a low Japanese-style table that sits over a small heater,
with a quilt draped between the frame and the top. It will be about 120 × 80 cm and 40 cm high,
made entirely of birch plywood. It's held together by interlocking joints only, with no glue,
nails or screws. The parts are cut by a computer-controlled router (CNC) from a file I prepare.
It's for my own home, not for sale.

**What I'm hoping for.** The tabletop is made of three boards that lock together along two seams.
Each seam is about 80 cm long. Instead of a plain joint, I'd love each seam to be a line you design,
so the joints themselves carry the artwork. Whatever subject and form you think is right is up to you.
I'd also really value your guidance on what is and isn't appropriate to put on a piece like this.

**What the line has to do (more detail in the attached PDF):**
- **Lock.** Somewhere along the seam, a shape has to be wider further in than at its opening, like a
  keyhole or dovetail, so the boards can't slide apart.
- **No sharp inside corners.** The cutter is round and about 6 mm (1/4") wide, so inside corners
  come out rounded.
- **No parts narrower than about 2.5 cm (1"),** so nothing cracks.
- **Stay within about 12 cm either side** of a straight line across the table.
- **It reads as a fine dark line** in pale birch. There's no colour, and no engraving unless you'd like
  some and we agree on it.

A pencil drawing on the attached sheet, photographed straight on, is plenty. A vector file is also
fine. I'll turn it into the cutting file myself and send you a proof (a drawing and a 3-D preview) to
approve. Nothing gets cut until you say it's right.

**Terms I'd propose (open to your changes):**
- **Fee:** [$___ for the design, or your rate], plus [$___] for an initial conversation, whether or not
  we go ahead.
- **Use:** one table, for my home only. The design isn't reproduced, sold or shared as a file. I'd
  keep the file private [or delete it after cutting / return it to you].
- **Credit:** however you'd like. That could be your name in my project notes, a small signature
  or mark on the underside, or nothing.
- **Photos:** I'd only share photos of the finished table with your permission, and credited the
  way you prefer.
- **Timeline:** no rush. Ideally [season / month], but your schedule comes first.
- **Afterwards:** I'd be glad to send you photos of the finished table, and an offcut of the seam if
  you'd like one.

**Questions I'd love your thoughts on:**
1. Would you be interested, and is this kind of use appropriate for your work?
2. Is there anything about the subject, placement or use (a table, with people's hands and cups on it)
   that I should know or respect?
3. Should the design come with a story or name that I keep with the table, or should it stand on its own?
4. What would your fee be, and what timeline works for you?

Thank you for considering it. I'm happy to meet in person, talk by phone, or just keep it to email,
whatever is easiest for you.

Kukwstsétsemc,
[your name]
[contact]
