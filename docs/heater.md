# The heater

I chose the **PaletteLife 120 V 400 W** replacement kotatsu heater. It runs on Canadian power, it's the
cheapest 120 V unit I found, DIY kotatsu builders rate it well, and it screws in at the sides the
traditional way. That's the one piece of hardware in the whole table. I researched this in October 2026 on
amazon.ca, delivering to rural BC. Prices are in CAD, including import fees where shown.

## What I needed
- **Native 120 V.** No 100 V Japanese unit, and no transformer.
- The usual kotatsu "replacement unit": a box about 29 cm square that screws in under the frame. Its own
  screws are the only hardware I allow.
- A fuse or thermal cut-off, and adjustable heat.
- Delivery to rural BC without phoning or emailing anyone.

## How it mounts
The listing's installation photo shows the heater between **two parallel crossbars**, held by **4 screws,
2 a side, driven sideways into the bars' inner faces** near the corners. In this table those bars are the
two heater cheeks hanging under the deck.

- I measured the body at 29 × 29 × 5 cm (to the nearest cm). The cheeks sit 294 mm apart, which leaves
  about 2 mm a side once it's centred.
- The cheeks run 41 mm past the heater at each end, so the corner screws go into solid wood at about half
  the cheek's 55 mm height. The tabs and wedges are on the top edge, out of the screws' way.

## Things to watch
- **Power.** 400 W was modest for my first 120 × 80 design. It's a better match for the 86 cm top. A thick
  futon, and a mat under it, help it warm up faster.
- **Certification.** Neither listing mentions a cUL, cETL or CSA mark. When it arrives, look for one on the
  rating label. If there isn't one, think about returning it within the 30 days, since it can matter for
  home insurance.
- **Airflow.** Don't block the fan or grille. Keep the futon and blankets off the unit, and follow the
  clearances in its manual. They override anything in this design.
